The Flow Formula

How Flow decides what to say next inside a thread. Nothing here is invented: the questions come from one book, the personality layer from another, and the glue from three published methods. Every thread, every person, every turn runs the same equation.

Turn = Reflect(what you just said, roof) + Question(stage, roof) · Rhythm(E|I) · Lens(T|F or J|P)
stage = the first of 7 script steps not yet answered    roof = temperament(16-letter type) ∈ {SP, SJ, NF, NT}
understood% = outcome·25 + challenge·25 + people·12.5 + timing·12.5 + dependencies·12.5 + motivation·12.5  → suggestions only at 100

Read it as: say back what you heard, then ask the next question in the script, worded for this person's temperament, at this person's rhythm.

01 The spine — seven questions, fixed order

Michael Bungay Stanier, The Coaching Habit (2016). His seven questions are the GROW model (John Whitmore, Coaching for Performance, 1992) compressed into words a lazy person can answer without thinking. Flow asks them in this order and never skips ahead. Each answer fills part of the thread's fingerprint.

1MINDWhat's on your mind?Coaching Habit · KickstartThe dump. Title, first facts.The record button. Never asked in words — it is the app.

02 The meter — when a thread is understood

The fingerprint has six points of understanding (the seventh, next, is the move — it comes after understanding, not before). Each point counts only with a quote from the person as evidence. Questions 3 and 4 are worth half the meter on their own, so the bar cannot reach 100 without both being answered — which is exactly the script's rule.

PointWeightFilled byEvidence looks like
outcome25Q4 WANT"show up Friday with the delay clearly not mine"
challenge25Q3 CHALLENGE"it looks like it's on me"
people12.5Q1 / Q2"my manager and his boss", "the designer"
timing12.5Q1 / Q2"2pm Friday"
dependencies12.5Q1 / Q2"the last two are with the designer"
motivation12.5Q4 (why-part) / Q2"the boss's boss is in the room"

Header wording: 0–99 "Getting to know this · n%" · 100 "Flow gets it". A thread also re-scores every time a note is added, so a point can be filled without ever being asked.

05 The dials — the two letters the roof doesn't use

Sandra Krebs Hirsh & Jane Kise, Introduction to Type and Coaching (CPP). A roof fixes two of the four letters. The other two don't change the words — they change the rhythm and the lens. This is what makes 16 types out of 4 scripts without inventing 16 scripts.

Rhythm · E / I — how many times Flow asks "And what else?"
EExtraverts think by talking (Hirsh & Kise). Keep pulling: up to 3 rounds, stop early when a round adds no new point. Flow's replies can be two bubbles. IIntroverts think first, then say the considered version. One "And what else?", then move on. One bubble per turn, shorter reflections, no stacking questions.
Lens · T / F — what Flow says back first (Artisans & Guardians: their J/P is fixed by the roof)
TReflect facts, sequence and logic first; people second. Hype names the result. FReflect people and what it means to them first; facts second. Hype names the person.
Lens · J / P — how the move is closed (Idealists & Rationals: their T/F is fixed by the roof)
JClosure. The move carries a date and a done-state, Q6 asks "by when", it lands on Today with a time. POpenness. The move is the smallest first step ("start, don't finish"), no date forced, Q6 asks what it displaces today.

08 Guardrails — what the formula forbids

No invented questions. Every question Flow asks is one of the seven, in one of the four wordings. The AI may reword nothing else.
No narrow drill-downs. Never "what exactly is missing", never "roughly when". Q2 does the pulling; if a point is still empty after Q4, Flow lives with it.
Reflect before you ask. Every question is preceded by one sentence saying back what was just said, in the person's words (MI reflective listening).
Nothing suggested below 100. The move exists only after the summary and after "How can I help?" is answered (MI: ask permission before advice).
One move. Built with GTD's two questions and stated as WOOP's if-then. Accepted by replying, never by a button.
The only buttons. "Its own thread? Yes / No" when a second subject appears — because the AI won't split correctly every time.
One open thing at a time. Never a question and an offer in the same turn. Never two questions.
Never echo the person's question back. If the person asked something, Flow answers from the thread, then continues the script.
